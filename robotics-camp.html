Six-day student curriculum

A focused robotics pathway students can continue at home.

Take-home activity booklet available to registered students

The six-day robotics plan has been converted into a take-home activity booklet for registered students, with guided activities that support hands-on building, beginner-friendly coding, testing, reflection, and practice beyond the camp session.

Day 1

Scratch Mission Game

Students model the tournament as a simple Scratch game and learn events, movement, boundaries, and logic.

Day 2

Arduino Nano + mBlock

Students meet the robot brain, connect snap-on electronics, and upload a successful hardware test.

Day 3

Movement + Calibration

Teams build the drivetrain, wire the motors, and tune forward, backward, left, and right movement.

Day 4

Phone Control

Students pair the robot by Bluetooth and complete safe driver drills using phone control commands.

Day 5

Gripper + Sensors

Teams mount the gripper, practise object pickup, explore sensor ideas, and run timed practice matches.

Day 6

Tournament + Showcase

Students complete final inspection, compete in the mini tournament, and present their design improvements.

Take-home workbook included

Registered students receive a printed booklet with daily goals, build steps, homework tasks, wiring diagrams, troubleshooting logs, tournament scoring sheets, and parent sign-off spaces.

Lead Instructor

Mr. Danual Bridgemohan

Mr. Danual Bridgemohan is a dedicated ICT educator, Head of IT at Waterloo Secondary School, and the founder of EduTech STEM Academy. He brings more than 20 years of classroom teaching, school technology leadership, and real technical support experience to the Robotics Camp.

His background combines education and industry: he has served as an ICT teacher and Head of IT, facilitated Ministry of Education ICT professional development for teachers, managed computer labs, supported school ICT infrastructure, and previously worked as a Business Systems Support Engineer providing PC, server, printer, operating-system, and network support for corporate clients.

Mr. Bridgemohan holds postgraduate qualifications in Education and Educational Technology with distinction, is currently pursuing an MSc in Cybersecurity at the University of Trinidad and Tobago, and has strong experience in website development, database design, Python, JavaScript, Java, C++, PC maintenance, networking, and server repair.

Proven robotics leadership

Building robotics culture at Waterloo Secondary School.

Club founded in 2019

The Waterloo Secondary School Robotics Club was established in 2019 by Mr. Danual Bridgemohan and Mr. Jerome Ramdahin to inspire students in robotics, engineering, computer science, and STEM education.

FIRST LEGO League success

When FIRST LEGO League came to Trinidad and Tobago, Waterloo competed nationally on two occasions and earned a trophy for outstanding performance before the programme was discontinued locally.

SUSTAINABOTS finalist

The club's innovative proposal was selected among the top twelve finalists in UTT's SUSTAINABOTS Competition, earning two Arduino Robotics Kits to support electronics and programming projects.

FTC Team Connect Award

On 27 June 2026, the club competed in the FIRST Tech Challenge against nine secondary schools and earned the Team Connect Award for collaboration, sportsmanship, and engagement with other teams.
